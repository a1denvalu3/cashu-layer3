01Claim binding & public issuanceAmount + backing hash+

Common keyset / BLS12-381

X2   = x · G2
Y_h2 = y_h · G2
Y_s2 = y_s · G2
Y_h1 = y_h · G1

H = SHA256(P)
h = int(SHA256(D || A_be64 || H)) mod q

D is the ASCII string Cashu_PS_HTLC_Claim_v2. A_be64 is the full amount in sats as 8-byte unsigned big-endian; H is 32 raw bytes. Digest conversion is big-endian. All scalar arithmetic is modulo q.

Mint signs the funded quote

C = s · G1
PoK{s : C = s · G1}

k ← fresh, secret, nonzero scalar
u = k · G1
v = k · [(x + y_h · h) · G1 + y_s · C]

The client submits its quote ID, C, and owner proof. The mint computes h from the fully accepted quote; the client cannot substitute its own h. One common keyset covers all amounts.

Why fresh k matters

If u = G1:     v★ = v + (h★ − h) · Y_h1
If u = k · G1: changing h requires y_h · u = k · Y_h1

A fixed signing base exposes a linear attribute forgery. Fresh secret k makes that correction a computational Diffie–Hellman problem in G1. Secret issuance randomness protects authenticity; later rerandomization serves privacy.

02Receiver verification & two bearer secretsAuthenticity ≠ live backing+

Offline authenticity check

U_s = s · u
e(v, G2) = e(u, X2 + h · Y_h2)
           · e(U_s, Y_s2)

Given u, v, h, U_s and the mint’s public key, anyone can check the pairing without s or P. The holder computes U_s locally. No separate “show” endpoint is needed.

The wallet recomputes h from the declared amount and H, verifies the signature, and checks SHA256(P) = H. Relabeling 1 sat as 1,000,000 sats breaks claim verification.

Inside the bearer token

cashuPS + base64url(JSON)

mint, version: 2, amount: A,
payment_hash: H, credential, preimage: P

s  → ownership and nullifier
P  → original Lightning settlement

The credential carries s. A swap replaces s and preserves P. A sender can retain copies of both secrets, so the receiver must swap to acquire a fresh spend credential.

A valid signature establishes what the mint signed. It does not establish that the hold is still accepted, that the nullifier is unspent, or that the mint will redeem.

03Private presentation & nullifierOne s, two public relations+

Randomized input credential

u′  = ρ · u
v″  = ρ · v + o · u′
κ_h = h · Y_h2 + o · G2
U_s = s · u′
N   = s · G_NULL

e(v″, G2) = e(u′, X2 + κ_h)
            · e(U_s, Y_s2)

Fresh nonzero ρ and o hide the input signature and claim attribute. The pairing checks the randomized credential’s authenticity. G_NULL is a fixed public hash-to-G1 base.

Owner / nullifier proof

PoK{s : N   = s · G_NULL
      ∧ U_s = s · u′}

Witness: s
Public: G_NULL, u′, N, U_s

r ← fresh scalar
T_N = r · G_NULL; T_s = r · u′
c = HashScalar(transcript)
z = r + c · s

The verifier reconstructs T_N = z · G_NULL − c · N and T_s = z · u′ − c · U_s, then checks c. The Fiat–Shamir transcript includes the domain, binding, bases, points, and nonce commitments.

Randomization leaves N unchanged. Replaying the credential with new ρ or o still hits the same spent-nullifier record. A successful swap consumes the old N atomically and issues a credential with a fresh owner secret and nullifier.

Owner/nullifier domain: Cashu_PS_Present_v1. Swap binding: Cashu_PS_HTLC_Swap_v2 || compressed(C_new). No P, H, amount, or unblinded h is transmitted in the swap.

04Same hidden claim, new ownerEquality proof + blind issuance+

The exact equality statement

B = h · Y_h1 + t · G1

PoK{(h, o, t) :
  κ_h = h · Y_h2 + o · G2
  ∧ B = h · Y_h1 + t · G1}

Witnesses: h, o, t
Public: κ_h, B, Y_h2, G2, Y_h1, G1

One shared witness h links the old credential’s masked attribute to the new signing commitment. The proof does not expose h or ask the mint to recompute it during swaps.

One request, one response

C_new = s_new · G1
PoK{s_new : C_new = s_new · G1}

U     = k · G1
V_raw = k · [x · G1 + B + y_s · C_new]
V     = V_raw − t · U

Output credential: (U, V, h, s_new)

The mint verifies the pairing, owner/nullifier proof, equality proof, and new owner proof; consumes N; and returns U and V_raw together. The client removes t · U locally.

Equality proof commitments

R_κ = r_h · Y_h2 + r_o · G2
R_B = r_h · Y_h1 + r_t · G1
z_h = r_h + c · h; z_o = r_o + c · o; z_t = r_t + c · t

Verifier reconstructs:
R_κ = z_h · Y_h2 + z_o · G2 − c · κ_h
R_B = z_h · Y_h1 + z_t · G1 − c · B

Equality domain: Cashu_PS_CommitEq_v1. Both the equality and owner/nullifier proofs bind C_new through the swap binding. The new owner PoK uses Cashu_PS_Issue_v1 and binds its own commitment through its transcript. POST /v1/swap carries version 2, the presentation, C_new, its owner proof, B, and the equality proof.

05Public burn & durable settlementPay → persist → settle+

Public claim, purpose-bound spend

H = SHA256(P)
h = ClaimScalar(A, H)
A = payout invoice amount

u′ = ρ · u; v′ = ρ · v
U_s = s · u′; N = s · G_NULL
e(v′, G2) = e(u′, X2 + h · Y_h2)
            · e(U_s, Y_s2)

The same owner/nullifier relation proves ownership. Its burn binding is Cashu_PS_HTLC_Redeem_v2 || A_be64 || SHA256(invoice_utf8), preventing substitution of the amount or payout invoice.

POST /v1/burn supplies A, the public-h presentation, the payout BOLT11, and original P. The mint checks the issued backing quote and its invoice state.

Two separate Lightning payments

Backing: H     = SHA256(P)
Payout:  H_out = SHA256(P_out)

reserve N, backing H, payout H_out
→ pay payout invoice
→ verify P_out and persist receipt
→ settle original hold with P
→ mark burn SETTLED / N SPENT

H_out can differ from H. The mint needs outgoing liquidity before the backing payment settles. Routing fees come from its own balance within its configured fee limit.

Uncertain payout results remain PENDING. A definite payout failure releases the reservation. A confirmed payout is recorded before hold settlement so exact retries can recover without paying twice. This recovery mechanism does not eliminate mint trust or HTLC expiry risk.

◇

Authenticity

The pairing verifies a credential under the mint’s key.

⊡

Spend authority

The proof connects s to N. The mint atomically enforces its state.

▥

Live backing

The hold must remain redeemable. The signature alone cannot prove this.

What this enables

[+]

Ownership moves above Lightning

Receivers acquire a fresh credential through the mint. Transfers do not settle the backing HTLC or create additional Lightning payments.

[+]

The mint sees a proof, not the claim

Swaps withhold h, H, amount, and P while proving that the same signed claim is preserved. This is protocol-level attribute privacy; timing and network correlation remain possible.

[+]

Amounts are authenticated

The mint signs its funded quote’s claim attribute. Wallets can detect false amount labels locally using one common keyset.

[+]

Settlement has a recovery path

Saved wallet operations, atomic nullifiers, durable payout receipts, and exact retries handle interrupted requests and settlement recovery.

What still needs work

[!]

The claim is indivisible

One whole HTLC per token. No splitting, combining, or change. Sending and burning must use the token’s full amount.

[!]

Backing has a lifetime

Held payments occupy channel liquidity and HTLC slots. Cancellation or timeout can remove backing while a signature continues to verify. Private swaps do not query the individual hidden backing hold.

[!]

Settlement remains a trust boundary

The mint controls issuance and payout, needs upfront outgoing liquidity, and covers fees. The protocol does not make redemption trustless. P remains known to prior holders even after s rotates.

[!]

Experimental protocol and cryptography

The Python mint and wallet implement this flow, with fake-backend and regtest tooling. The custom cashuPS encoding is incompatible with ordinary Cashu wallets. Cross-mint redemption is absent; production hardening and independent review remain necessary.

Open research / fractional control

Splitting a credential
doesn’t split the HTLC.

A future design must conserve spendable value and define partial redemption. LND settles the whole original hold with P. Settling it on the first partial burn changes the backing of any remainder; delaying settlement requires the mint to front payouts and manage expiry. No fractional scheme is implemented or selected here.
